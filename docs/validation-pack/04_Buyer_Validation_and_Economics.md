# Buyer evidence before more features

No interviews, named prospects, willingness to pay or commitments have been obtained here. The tracker is intentionally header-only. Populate it with actual observations and consented contact information; do not create dummy customer rows for a pitch.

## First buyer hypothesis

Start with a program lead for practical business/operations training who owns or can reach a budget and currently pays staff to inspect whether work is correct. A second segment to compare is an employer training lead onboarding junior operations staff. Access through Scott's experience may shorten discovery but does not establish authorized distribution.

Do not start by selling to frontier labs. First prove task quality and assessment usefulness with practitioners, then show the environment's contribution to research reviewers. Treat training-product sales and research relevance as separate evidence streams.

## Ten questions for a 20-minute interview

1. Tell me about the last time you assessed a learner or new hire doing an actual operations workflow.
2. What records did they need to update, and how did you establish the outcome was correct?
3. Describe a recent case where an excellent written answer concealed incomplete or incorrect work.
4. What changed during that work, and how did the participant respond?
5. How many staff minutes did assessment and follow-up take? Can we measure that rather than estimate it?
6. What tool or approach do you currently use, including free job simulations or manual exercises?
7. What would make a synthetic scenario too unrealistic to use in your setting?
8. Who owns the budget, when can it be committed, and what approvals are required?
9. Would you review one task and rubric with us, and what would you need to see before considering a paid pilot?
10. Which specific outcome would justify continuing after a pilot, and what would cause you to stop?

Ask about past behavior before describing features or a price. Record direct observations separately from interviewee estimates and interpretation. A compliment, demo request or informal interest is not a commercial commitment.

## A bounded offer to test

Propose a six-week pilot for one operations role, the three existing families and six episodes, up to 25 consenting participants, one onboarding session, assessor onboarding, a results review and a redacted evidence export. This is an offer hypothesis, not an available delivery commitment before hosted/privacy readiness.

Starting price experiment: CAD 3,000 for the bounded scope, before applicable taxes. Do not promise unlimited authoring or integrations. A higher quoted price in the original CAD 3,000–7,500 hypothesis should be tied to actual additional scope and capacity, not asserted market value. Confirm whether the customer requires SSO, LMS or region-specific hosting before committing; these can change delivery economics and are not automatically included.

Suggested pilot success criteria to negotiate: completion of agreed practitioner task review; observed review effort compared with the actual current process; interpretable state-based evidence; acceptable participant access/usability; no committed integrity or privacy incident; and a budget owner's concrete renewal decision. No promise of hiring suitability, accreditation or universal learning gains.

## Unit economics to measure

Use: pilot contribution = cash revenue less variable platform/inference costs, delivery/authoring/support labor and directly attributable acquisition cost. Record reusable product engineering separately so neither it nor bespoke service work disappears. Price internal labor at an explicit planning rate; CAD 100/hour is an illustrative sensitivity value, not a market wage estimate.

At CAD 3,000, CAD 150 variable costs and CAD 100/hour labor:

| Total attributable labor | Contribution before fixed costs and tax | Contribution percentage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 12 hours | CAD 1,650 | 55.0% |
| 24 hours | CAD 450 | 15.0% |
| 36 hours | CAD -750 | -25.0% |

These are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts. To target 50% contribution at those assumed values, attributable labor must be at most 13.5 hours. If a real pilot needs 30 hours of bespoke tasks, either raise price for that scope, charge separately for authoring, narrow customization or treat the offer as a service learning experiment. Do not call it scalable because the app is hosted.

Log authoring, assessor support, onboarding, integrations, issue handling and sales time separately. The second similar deployment should need materially less labor. Track scenario reuse and policy-only adaptations; expanding the task catalog is valuable only when maintenance and review costs remain favorable.

## Ten-interview decision checkpoint

After ten relevant conversations, use the following management rules as proposed thresholds, not statistical proof:

- Continue to a paid-pilot proposal if at least three independently describe the same concrete assessment/recovery pain and at least two budget owners identify a credible paid-pilot approval path.
- Prioritize a pilot only when scope, timing, privacy and delivery labor are bounded. Track the difference between verbal interest, signed agreement and cash collected.
- Narrow the buyer segment if the pain is concentrated in one role/program.
- Rework the proposition if most need an LMS replacement, interview coach or content library rather than operational evidence.
- Stop adding commercial features if no budget owner sees value beyond an existing/free alternative. Keep the bounded research artifact if it remains scientifically useful.
